## Lead (short narrative entry)

Luke’s second volume ends with Paul in Rome under guard, welcoming all who come, “proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance” (Acts 28:30–31). There is no courtroom finale, no sword, no Spain itinerary narrated. Visitors who ask “what happened next?” deserve an honest answer: **Scripture stops here**—then early Christian memory and evangelical reconstruction pick up with sources and confidence labels, not with invented Bible verses.

This essay is the late-Paul porch for that open ending. It presents forks you can inspect. It does not force a single cinematic martyrdom as “what the Bible says.”

## 1. Where Acts goes quiet

**Working label:** Where Acts Goes Quiet

**What Scripture gives**

| Source | What it gives | What it does not settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acts 28:16–31 | Paul in Rome, rented quarters, under guard; two years of open proclamation | No martyrdom scene; no release narrated; no Spain trip narrated |
| Romans 15:24, 28 | Paul’s clear **plan** to go on to Spain after Rome and the collection | Whether that plan was later fulfilled |
| 2 Timothy 4:6–8, 16–18 | Farewell tone—“poured out as a drink offering,” crown of righteousness, the Lord will bring him safely into his heavenly kingdom—(Consensus: late Pauline charge) | No named emperor, no beheading narrative, no calendar year |

**Scripture line for the UI:** Acts ends with the gospel unhindered in Rome. The New Testament does not narrate Paul’s death.

**Consensus framing already used on this project:** Prison Letters with Acts 28 Rome house arrest; Pastorals after a release; 2 Timothy from a final, harsher imprisonment. Wider Protestant notes exist on prison-letter provenance and Pastoral timing elsewhere in the atlas—do not reopen late-authorship schemes here.

## 2. Branch: Rome and the sword

**Working label:** Rome and the sword  
**Confidence:** Early tradition (strong memory) → legendary elaboration (low as raw history)

**Summary.** Evangelical Consensus holds that Paul was martyred in Rome under Nero in the mid-60s after Acts. That reconstruction rests on (1) 2 Timothy’s readiness for death, (2) early church memory, and (3) the usual chronology of Nero’s reign—not on a New Testament execution scene.

**Sources (label each in UI chips — required):** Scripture · Early tradition · Legendary · Wider / later memory.

**Sources:**

- **2 Timothy 4:6–8, 16–18** — *Scripture.* Farewell tone and trust that the Lord will rescue him “into his heavenly kingdom.” Implies approaching death for many readers; does not name Nero or describe a sword.
- **1 Clement 5** (c. AD 96) — *Early tradition.* Remembers Paul’s endurance and martyrdom; speaks of reaching the “furthest limits of the west” (see Spain branch for the interpretive debate).
- **Acts of Paul** (2nd century) — *Legendary narrative, low confidence as history.* Beheading under Nero; famous motifs (including “milk instead of blood”) belong to pious storytelling, not to Acts.
- **Later church memory** — Rome under Nero as the dominant traditional ending; feast association with Peter on June 29 (memory, not a biblical calendar).

**Dating note:** Consensus window c. AD 64–67. Wider Protestant nuance: some prefer nearer AD 64 (after the fire and Neronian persecution), others nearer AD 67 (toward Nero’s end). Exact year within the mid-60s is not fixed by Scripture.

**Authoring rule:** Never present “beheaded under Nero” as a Bible verse. Present it as **tradition illuminating what Acts leaves open**, with confidence marked.

**Deep-links:** `final-imprisonment-2timothy`, `martyrdom-nero`; city `rome`.

## 3. Branch: The Spanish question

**Working label:** The Spanish question  
**Confidence:** Plan = Scripture (clear). Fulfillment = Wider Protestant / contested tradition.

**Summary.**

- **Plan attested:** Paul writes that he hopes to see the Romans in passing “and to be helped on my journey there by you, once I have enjoyed your company for a while,” and again that when he has delivered the collection he will go on “by way of you to Spain” (Romans 15:24, 28).
- **Acts silent on fulfillment:** Luke never narrates a western journey beyond Rome.
- **Consensus on this site:** Release after the Acts 28 biennium is the usual reconstruction (`release-spain-plan`); Romans records the desire and plan.
- **Wider Protestant only:** Whether Paul later **fulfilled** Spain—often argued from 1 Clement’s “limit of the west” and later writers—is post-Acts inference, not New Testament narrative.

Do not force visitors to pick Spain “yes” or “no” as doctrine. Show the plan as Scripture; show fulfillment as an open fork with sources.

**Deep-links:** `release-spain-plan`, `pastorals-1tim-titus`; Romans 15; city `rome`.

## 4. Branch: Relics and memory

**Working label:** Relics and memory  
**Confidence:** Archaeological / liturgical memory of the church — not a substitute for Acts

**Summary.** Later Christian memory locates Paul’s burial tradition along the Via Ostiense at what became the Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls. The shared traditional feast with Peter on **June 29** marks how the church remembered the apostles in Rome. These sites and dates teach reception history—how believers honored Paul—without rewriting Luke’s open ending or supplying a missing martyrdom chapter.

**Use in UI:** short panel + optional external reading links; always subordinate to Acts 28 and 2 Timothy.

**Deep-links:** city `rome`; timeline `martyrdom-nero` (display title: **End of Paul (tradition after Acts)**).
