# What Others Say About Paul (parked)

**Status:** Content still parked (no full essays). First-visit IA + `#/voices` surface a lightweight stub: six voice cards with one-liners and Scripture links only.

**Module working title:** What Others Say About Paul

**Audience need:** A reference frame for how other Scripture figures (and named opponents) viewed Paul’s character, thinking, and the early church’s tensions around him.

**Goal:** Cultural / theological frame of who Paul was *to those around him* — not another biography of Paul alone.

---

## Proposed structure (UI later)

**Index:** “Voices on Paul”

Each figure (or group) is an **entry card** with:

- One-line summary
- Passage links (ESV default; NASB available per site framing)
- Optional short expandable note (authored later)
- Links into letters / Acts / timeline where relevant

---

## Outline sections (with source passages)

### 1. Peter

- **Passages:** 2 Peter 3:15–16
- **One-line summary (draft):** Calls Paul’s letters “scripture,” yet flags them as hard to understand and open to distortion by the unstable.
- **Frame:** Affirmation + caution in the same breath — canonical respect without pretending every reader found Paul easy.

### 2. James and the Jerusalem circle

- **Passages:** Acts 15 (Jerusalem Council); Acts 21:17–26 (Paul reports to James); Galatians 2:9–10 (pillars gave the right hand of fellowship)
- **One-line summary (draft):** Recognition and negotiation — fellowship for the Gentile mission alongside ongoing concern for Jewish believers and practice.
- **Frame:** Not a cartoon split; Council, later visit, and Galatians’ “pillars” as complementary windows. Mark Consensus vs Wider only where dating of Gal 2 / Acts already differs elsewhere on the site.

### 3. Luke / Acts

- **Passages:** Portrait across Acts, including the “we” passages
- **One-line summary (draft):** Sympathetic companion narrative — not hagiography; conflict, arrest, and defense scenes stay in view.
- **Frame:** Primary narrative spine for travel and conflict; distinct from Paul’s own letters as a second voice.

### 4. Jude

- **Passages:** Jude 17–18 (echoes 2 Peter 3:3); debated link to Pauline tradition
- **One-line summary (draft):** Shared early-church warning language; any direct “about Paul” link stays labeled as debated, not settled.
- **Frame:** Include for completeness of “other voices”; confidence low on a tight Pauline tie unless later review raises it.

### 5. Opponents

- **Passages:** 2 Corinthians 10–13; Galatians 1:6–9; Philippians 1:15–18
- **One-line summary (draft):** Rivals cast Paul as crafty, bold on paper, weak in person — while some preach Christ from envy; Paul answers without erasing the conflict.
- **Frame:** Let opponents’ charges appear as *reported* charges (often via Paul’s rebuttal), not as neutral biography. Useful for “how Paul sounded to critics.”

### 6. Paul on himself (contrast panel)

- **Passages:** 1 Corinthians 15:9; Ephesians 3:8; 1 Timothy 1:15
- **One-line summary (draft):** Least of the apostles / least of all saints / foremost of sinners — self-assessment set beside external praise, caution, and attack.
- **Frame:** Not “others,” but a deliberate contrast column so visitors hear external views against Paul’s own humility language. Apply existing letter-authorship / dating notes where the site already distinguishes Consensus vs Wider.

## Authoring rules (when un-parked)

- Scripture authoritative; Consensus evangelical framing; ESV default.
- Distinguish **what a text says** from **later reconstruction**.
- Opponents’ views appear as claims in the text, not site endorsements.
- Jude’s Pauline connection stays marked debated unless independent review upgrades confidence.
- Deep-link to letter compare, Acts timeline events, and Rome / Jerusalem city packs where helpful.

---

## Anti-patterns

- Flattening Peter, James, and Paul into a single “they all agreed” slide
- Treating 2 Corinthians’ opponents as cartoon villains without quoting the charges
- Presenting Jude as a clear Pauline witness without the debate label
- Building UI before passage cards and summaries are reviewed
